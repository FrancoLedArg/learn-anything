# Learn Anything

An AI tutor in the browser that teaches students any subject the way a good human teacher does: by constantly diagnosing what the student understands and adapting to how they learn.

## People

**Student**:
A person learning with the app.
_Avoid_: Learner, user

**Research consent**:
A student's agreement that their lessons may be used in research. Students under 18 also need a guardian's agreement.
_Avoid_: Data consent, opt-in

## Understanding

**Understanding**:
A student's ability to relate pieces of information to each other. One right answer, or an answer given with confidence, is not enough to show it. It shows only through repeated evidence of different kinds.
_Avoid_: Knowing, mastery

**Fading**:
The loss of understanding that is not exercised. Something a student understood before cannot be assumed to still be understood.
_Avoid_: Forgetting, decay

**Check**:
A moment where the tutor asks the student to show they understood, such as a practice scenario or a question in the middle of an explanation. Checks come in different kinds, and understanding needs several of them.
_Avoid_: Test, quiz, exam

**Knowledge floor**:
The point where a student's understanding of a topic ends. It is where teaching has to start.
_Avoid_: Baseline, starting level

## Teaching

**Diagnosis**:
The tutor's ongoing work of finding out what the student understands and whether the teaching is working. The tutor asks the student for answers before and throughout a lesson, not only at the end.
_Avoid_: Assessment, probing, measuring

**Student profile**:
How a particular student learns best, for example their friction to difficulty, or whether they prefer more examples or more practice.
_Avoid_: Learner profile, learning style

**Digital twin**:
The app's model of one student: their student profile and what they understand. Every check corrects it, so it gets closer to the real student over time.
_Avoid_: Student model, user profile

**Knowledge graph**:
The part of a digital twin that maps what a student understands: the pieces, how they relate, and how strongly each is held right now. The student can browse their own graph.
_Avoid_: Second brain, knowledge map

**Friction to difficulty**:
How much ramp-up a student needs before taking on harder material. A high-friction student may need to watch it done first. A low-friction student jumps straight to the hardest thing. The same applies to technical vocabulary versus plain words.
_Avoid_: Difficulty preference, pace
